Antonelli vs Norris - Madrid

An Analysis of Their Quali Battle

Posted by Yeeva Tsewang on September 12, 2026
Formula 1's newest venue brought an exciting qualifying spectacle. The Madring is the newest track on the calendar, and all 22 drivers had to learn the ropes and adapt to the circuit's complexities. The sights of drivers catching bouts of snap oversteer and grazing the barriers on corner exits were especially common. Competition among the frontrunners was tight, with the top five finishers separated by less than two tenths of a second. However, the fight on the front row of the grid was the tightest, with only 0.011 seconds separating Lando Norris in P1 with Kimi Antonelli in P2. Below, we will break down their final Q3 laps and analyze where Norris got those extra milliseconds.

Sector 1

Going into turn 1, Antonelli carries a speed advantage of 5 km/h. They brake around the same time, but Norris maintains a slightly higher speed through the corner and gets on throttle slightly earlier. However, the Mercedes' power causes Antonelli to close the gap. At the 1,045 meter mark, both drivers experience clipping through turn 4. At its peak, Norris's advantage in turn 4 reaches 10 km/h. As they brake into turns 5 and 5A (1,500 - 1,575 m), Antonelli maintains a speed advantage. However, Norris gets a smoother exit out of turn 5A (1,575 m). Despite this, right before they brake going into turn 7, Antonelli has a 14 km/h lead (1,810 m).

Sector 2

Going into turn 7, Antonelli maintains a speed advantage, but Norris gets on power earlier at the exit of turn 8. On the run down turn 9, a long right hander, and into turn 10 (the stretch from 1,965 - 2,200 m), Norris maintains a small speed advantage. Accelerating through turn 11 (2,370 - 2,455 m), he keeps this lead. He enters the banked turn 12 faster (2,580 - 2,665 m), but Antonelli closes the gap on the exit and down the run to turn 13 (2,735 - 3,150 m). The winner of turn 13 is Norris, who maintains a higher apex speed and gets on power quicker. From 3,290 meters all the way to 3,825 meters, which is the run from turn 13 exit to turn 16 entry, Norris maintains the speed advantage. Going into turn 17, however, Antonelli brakes later.

Sector 3

Although he enters turn 17 quicker, Antonelli loses to Norris on the exit, who (as usual) gets on throttle earlier. He keeps this speed gain up to turn 18's entry (4,125 m). This only requires a slight lift from the drivers. Although Norris gets an early advantage, Antonelli takes the speed lead from turn 18 to the entry of turn 20. As was the story for turn 17, he enters turn 20 (4,675 - 4,750 m) quicker, but Norris has a faster exit which he maintains going through turn 20A and into turn 21's entry (4,800 m). He keeps the lead from the exit until the entry of turn 22. Antonelli gets a better run on the exit and keeps the advantage until the start/finish line.

Conclusion

The data highlights the advantage Norris possesses in the corners, and while the Mercedes of Antonelli attempts to catch him using raw straight-line speed, it just is not enough. Although he enters corners faster, Kimi keeps losing out on the exits. It will be interesting to see how the race plays out as both drivers attempt to maximize the performance out of their cars.
