Drastic Situations Call for Drastic Measures

Just How Much Was Lando Norris Lifting-And-Coasting?

Posted by Yeeva Tsewang on November 29, 2025

After a shocking double disqualification for McLaren, Max Verstappen now stands equal on points with Oscar Piastri and only 24 points behind Lando Norris. Both McLarens were disqualified due to excess skid block wear. It seems that the team was aware of this issue during the race, instructing Norris to lift-and-coast at the end of the straights.

However, just how much was Norris LICO(Lift-and-Coast)-ing? Prior to being disqualified, Norris finished 20.741 seconds behind Verstappen. At first glance, this huge gap could be explained away by Verstappen's pure racecraft and pace. This, however, is not taking into account the extreme lifting Norris was performing as the race went on.

Analysis

To get an accurate comparison of the time delta between Norris and Verstappen, we first get the laps where the former was P2 and the latter was P1. These were from laps 17 to 22 and 34 to 50 (the end of the race). The time delta after each lap can then be found and plotted. This is shown in Figure 1.

Figure 1. A scatterplot of the time delta between Verstappen in P1 and Norris in P2. The gap in the middle with no data points represents instances when Norris was not in P2.

We see a steady increase in the gap between the drivers up until lap 46, which displays a sudden spike. This spike increases after each successive lap until lap 50. We can track this pattern with telemetry data, which effectively conveys the story of the race.

Lap 17

Figure 2. Comparison of Norris vs Verstappen's telemetry on lap 17 (The first lap where Norris is P2 and Verstappen is P1).

Notice here that Norris is still pushing, with no signs of lifting-and-coasting. Take note of turns 11 and 17 and how both drivers' speed are similar.

Lap 25

Figure 3. Comparison of Norris vs Verstappen's telemetry on lap 25.

Lap 25 is the first obvious instance of Norris LICO-ing, namely in turns 11 and 17, circled in black. Note that Verstappen's sudden drop in speed at turn 17 is because he pitted at the end of this lap and therefore had to decrease his speed to maintain the pit lane speed limit.

Lap 40

Figure 4. Comparison of Norris vs Verstappen's telemetry on lap 40.

On this lap we see Norris performing more aggressive lifting into turn 11, as shown by the steeper drop in speed than on lap 25. His lifting on turn 17 is around the same amount as on lap 25.

Lap 46

Figure 5. Comparison of Norris vs Verstappen's telemetry on lap 46.

This lap displays a natural progression from lap 40, with Norris lifting more on both corners.

Lap 47

Figure 6. Comparison of Norris vs Verstappen's telemetry on lap 47.

This is the where the lifting becomes very aggressive. Not only is Norris lifting well ahead of turn 11 by several hundred meters and sharply getting off the throttle into turn 17, he's also starting to lift going into turn 14, circled in green. We also see first evidence of lifting into turns 5 and 10, circled in yellow and red respectively.

Lap 48

Figure 7. Comparison of Norris vs Verstappen's telemetry on lap 48.

Going into turns 5, 10, 11, 14, and 17, Norris is lifting even more aggressively. He starts lifting on the straights going into turns 13 and 14, circled in blue and green respectively. There is also a small lift going into turn 7, circled in red.

Lap 50

Figure 8. Comparison of Norris vs Verstappen's telemetry on lap 50.

On the last lap, Norris lifts less going into turns 10, 11, 13, 14, and 17. He also stops lifting on the straight going into turn 14. His lift into turn 5 is about the same. By comparison, his lift into turn 7 is more drastic than his lift on lap 48, lifting right after the apex of turn 6.

Takeaways

As we can see from Figure 1 and the corresponding telemetry sheets, the spike in time delta starting at lap 46 is explained by Norris's extreme LICO-ing. On the preceding laps, the speed difference ranges from around 10-20 kph. After lap 46, the range is approximately between 25-60 kph. Although this LICO-ing wasn't enough to mitigate the skid block wear, it's interesting to see how drastic the progression became.
