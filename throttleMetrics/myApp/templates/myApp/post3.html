Head-To-Head During Abu Dhabi Qualifying

An Analysis Between Verstappen and Norris

Posted by Yeeva Tsewang on December 6, 2025

Qualifying for the 2025 Abu Dhabi Grand Prix gave us a familiar sight: Max Verstappen, Lando Norris, and Oscar Piastri qualifying in the top 3. This post will zoom in on the top two.

Verstappen and Norris were only separated by two tenths of a second, and we can analyze their telemetry (Figure 1) to see where this difference comes from. It's best if we do this on a sector basis.

Figure 1. A telemetry comparison between the fastest lap of Verstappen and Norris during qualifying.

It might also be useful to compare their throttle and brake inputs. This is shown below in Figure 2.

Figure 2. A comparison between Verstappen and Norris's throttle and braking inputs on their fastest Q3 time.

Sector 1 | Time (seconds) - Verstappen: 16.858; Norris: 16.908

Figure 3. Comparison of Verstappen vs Norris's sector 1 quali telemetry. The x-axis is labeled by meters. Figure 4. Comparison of Verstappen vs Norris's sector 1 quali throttle and braking inputs. The x-axis is labeled by meters.

Throughout sector 1, Verstappen maintains a marginal speed advantage over Norris. Going into turn 1, he gets off 100% throttle and brakes just a bit later than Norris, around the 300 meter mark. Norris, however, has a higher minimum speed through turn 1 and gets on throttle slightly earlier than Verstappen. From the 400 meter mark up to the 900 meter mark, he holds a speed advantage over Verstappen. Besides Norris getting on throttle earlier, this could be due to Verstappen's shaky acceleration just before the 500 meter mark. From the 900 meter mark to the end of the sector, however, Verstappen closes the gap and claims the advantage.

Sector 2 | Time (seconds) - Verstappen: 35.710; Norris: 35.890

Figure 5. Comparison of Verstappen vs Norris's sector 2 quali telemetry. The x-axis is labeled by meters. Figure 6. Comparison of Verstappen vs Norris's sector 2 quali throttle and braking inputs. The x-axis is labeled by meters.

Going into turn 5, Verstappen gets off throttle earlier and gets on the brakes earlier. Exiting out of turn 5, Norris gets off the brakes sooner which allows him to have a higher minimum speed at the apex, but gets on throttle later. On the run from turn 5's exit to turn 6's entry, Verstappen holds a speed advantage over Norris which grows over the course of the straight.

Going into turn 6, Verstappen gets off throttle several meters later than Norris, just past the 2500 meter mark. He also gets on the brakes a couple meters after Norris. They both get off the brakes at practically the same time just past the 2600 meter mark, though Norris gets on throttle earlier. Verstappen lifts off the throttle later than Norris going into turn 7. Exiting turn 7, Norris gets on throttle earlier. From turn 7 exit to turn 8, it seems that Verstappen has trouble putting the power down as his speed curve contains blips. Past turn 8 (which really is just a slight bend), he gradually pulls away from Norris, increasing his speed advantage until the end of the sector.

Sector 3 | Time (seconds) - Verstappen: 29.639; Norris: 29.610

Figure 7. Comparison of Verstappen vs Norris's sector 3 quali telemetry. The x-axis is labeled by meters. Figure 8. Comparison of Verstappen vs Norris's sector 3 quali throttle and braking inputs. The x-axis is labeled by meters.

This is the first sector where Norris has a faster time, though only by 0.029 seconds. Going into turn 9, Verstappen gets off throttle earlier and gets on the brakes later, with the former by several meters and the latter only marginally. Exiting turn 9, Norris gets on throttle earlier. Verstappen also needs to lift slightly from the ~3680 meter-mark to the ~3780 meter mark.

Turns 12 to 16, which are all relatively low speed corners, are where Norris makes up speed. He's able to get on throttle earlier at the exit of turn 12. Although Verstappen matches his speed through turn 13, Norris gets a better exit at turn 14. This, along with the fact that Verstappen had to blip the brakes just before turn 14 around the 4500 meter-mark, caused Norris to maintain the speed advantage from turn 14 exit to turn 16 entry. Verstappen makes up for this by getting off throttle later at turn 16 entry, though he and Norris get on the brakes at the same time. At turn 16 exit, he gets off the brakes slightly earlier and gets on throttle around the same time as Norris.

Takeaways

The telemetry paints a clear picture: Red Bull have opted for a high-speed, low drag package to try to keep the McLarens at bay on the straights and medium-high speed corners. It will be interesting to see how this plays out, especially in sector 3 where the McLarens can maintain higher speeds. This advantage, however, could be very minimal since, as seen in the telemetry, it only gained them 0.029 seconds. Still, anything is possible, and we will need to wait and see how the race plays out.
