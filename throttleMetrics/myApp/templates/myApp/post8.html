How Much Does Track Experience Matter?

Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia Quali Performance Analysis

Posted by Yeeva Tsewang on October 3, 2026

The Sepang International Circuit returns to the F1 calendar for the first time since 2017 to host the Bahrain Grand Prix, which was cancelled (along with the Saudi Arabian Grand Prix) earlier in the season. Across the years that Sepang hosted the Malaysian Grand Prix, it produced extremely competitive, tight, and close racing. It's also been the source of some of F1's most iconic moments and battles, such as the tension-flaring Multi-21 incident between Sebastian Vettel and Mark Webber in 2013. Because of the circuit's F1 pedigree, when its return was announced, it was met with widespread excitement by both the F1 community and drivers.

Of the current 22 driver grid, 10 of them have previously raced there. For the other 12, it'll be their first time racing at the circuit. I found that this dynamic created an interesting situation since those other 12 drivers have had no race experience at the circuit and have needed to rely on simulator and free practice runs to get to grips with the track. Because of this, would those 10 drivers who do have race experience have an advantage? And if so, how much? In a similar approach to my previous impact analysis post, I'll attempt to quantify the average lap time gained in Qualifying associated with previous Sepang race experience.

Gathering and Prepping Data

The data for this analysis was obtained from the FastF1 and Ergast API. FastF1 was used to get the session's lap times. An important note is that in-laps, out-laps, cooldown laps, and outlier laps were filtered out because these are not reflective of a driver-and-car's true performance. For instance, pole-sitter Max Verstappen had many such slow laps, like on lap 3, 6, and 12 (which were likely cooldown laps). If these laps weren't filtered out, they would confuse the model since they're not reflective of his true qualifying pace.

Ergast was used to extract a crucial historical variable for each driver: racedBefore. This is a dummy variable and is the predictor we're concerned about. An important note is that I only counted drivers with a Malaysian Grand Prix race start as True, so although Charles Leclerc did an FP1 session at Sepang in 2017, he is marked as False.

Now that the data is obtained and transformed, the regression modeling can start.

Regression Modeling

As with my previous impact analysis, I used three models for this multiple linear regression: Ordinary Least Squares (OLS), Lasso, and Elastic Net. The R-style equation I used is below. The predictor we are concerned about is \[\text{racedBefore}\] To isolate the effects of on-track race experience, I have added additional covariates to account for confounding variables.

\[ \text{LapTimeS} \sim \text{racedBefore} + \text{C(Team)} + \text{Stint} + \text{TyreLife} \]
Variable Meaning Interpretation
racedBefore Dummy variable for Sepang race experience Lap-time difference associated with race experience at Sepang
C(Team) The team that the driver races for Controls for persistent team-level performance differences
Stint What stint the driver was on Controls for stint-specific conditions
TyreLife The condition of the driver's tires for a given lap Controls for tire wear effects

Results

After fitting, the results were as follows:

Model Estimate Confidence Interval (95%)
OLS -0.272 [-0.504, -0.040]
Lasso -0.272 [-0.504, -0.040]
Elastic Net -0.272 [-0.504, -0.040]

For OLS, I achieved a best estimate for racedBefore of -0.272, and this was statistically significant, with a p-value of 0.022 (less than 0.05). This estimate and accompanying confidence interval remained consistent for Lasso and Elastic Net. Therefore, I estimate that prior Sepang race experience is associated with an average lap-time improvement of 0.272 seconds, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.04 to 0.504 seconds, controlling for team, stint, and tyre life.
